# Heart & Soul — subtraction doctrine + the awwwards/Collins bar (v2, 2026-09-05)

## Core
Perfection: nothing left to remove without harming the user's ability to succeed. Minimum interface around maximum capability.
Essential path: **Enter → Act → Review → Complete → Recover/Reuse.** Every screen earns its place on that path or leaves.
Prefer *system detects → prepares → user confirms* over *user configures → manages → fixes*.
Human control at the right moment: consequential, ambiguous, costly, or anything shared / paid / published / executed.

## SYNTHIA's one sentence
*"See what the council decided, approve what needs me, and trust the rest ran."* Everything else is progressive disclosure.

## The subtraction test (run before approving any surface)
Remove? Combine? Infer? Show only when needed? Understandable without explanation? Fewer decisions? Advanced capability elsewhere? Stop only when the next removal harms usefulness, confidence, accessibility, or control.

## Decision hierarchy
outcome > clarity > trust > ease > speed > accessibility > consistency > delight > polish > novelty. Never trade up for down.

## The bar: awwwards-worthy AND Collins-level (both, not either)
awwwards judges on design 40 · usability 30 · creativity 20 · content 10. Collins demands a governing idea, distinction, proof, self-evidence, systems, sovereignty. SYNTHIA must clear both — which means:
1. **One governing idea per surface** (Observatorio: you read the sky to know the company). If a screen can't state its idea in a sentence, it isn't designed yet.
2. **Signature moment, not signature everywhere.** One memorable interaction per journey (coherence lock → memo materializes). Everything around it is quiet.
3. **Motion is data.** Every animation maps to a field value (energy, phase, coherence, speaking) or a decision (approval, gate). Idle screens do not move. Reduced-motion is a first-class path, not a fallback.
4. **Typography carries hierarchy; color carries state.** Sphere colors are the only saturation; slate→amber is the only alarm. No gradients as decoration.
5. **Real content shapes layout.** Spanish first, long words, diacritics at display size, tabular numerals for money in MXN/EUR/USD.
6. **Self-evident in 5 seconds** (Krug): where am I, what is this, what do I do next, did it work. Test with a native es-MX user who has never seen the app.
7. **Proof before claims.** No fake metrics, testimonials, or status dots. If the number isn't real, the element is deleted.
8. **Craft floor**: 60 fps on the meeting screen; INP < 200 ms; no horizontal overflow at 390 px; WCAG AA contrast; keyboard reachable; focus visible; Lighthouse ≥ 90 on idle pages.
9. **Cliché blacklist enforced** (Collins): centered generic hero, purple-blue gradients, glassmorphism, floating dashboard mockups, three-card feature rows, default bento, pill-everything, random luxury serif, stock lifestyle photos, decorative 3D spheres, marquee, parallax without narrative, template testimonials, AI-brain imagery. Our spheres pass only because they *mean* something.
10. **Hard to imitate honestly.** The system is distinctive because of the council + voice + physics, not because of a color palette. A competitor copying the CSS would still have dead orbs.

## First-run rule
The first 60 seconds must produce one true outcome (a council speaks about *your* business) before any settings, menus, or pricing appear. Tour overlays explain what just happened, never what could happen.

## Non-negotiables
Do not expose complexity because it exists · do not add a feature without the pain it removes · do not automate consequential decisions without review · do not keep a feature because engineering built it · do not hide information needed for trust · do not require users to understand the architecture · do not ship a screen you cannot A/B against the bar.
